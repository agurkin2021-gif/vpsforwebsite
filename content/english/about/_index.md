## What this site does

VPSforWebsite.com is an independent practical guide for people deciding whether a website needs a VPS and, if it does, how much CPU, RAM and storage to start with.

The site is not built around fixed visitor-count promises. A cached brochure site, a plugin-heavy WordPress site and a WooCommerce checkout can create very different server workloads at the same traffic level.

## How recommendations are built

We separate **provider facts** from **editorial sizing guidance**.

Provider facts include published CPU, RAM, storage, traffic allowance and price. Workload labels such as “small website,” “business WordPress” or “heavier WooCommerce” are our interpretation of how those resources can fit common website workloads.

Sizing guidance considers:

- PHP worker concurrency;
- database activity;
- background jobs and scheduled tasks;
- caching behavior;
- control-panel overhead;
- storage growth;
- several sites sharing one VPS;
- operational headroom for updates, backups and recovery.

## What we do not claim

We do not publish a fixed number of visitors that a VPS can “handle” as if traffic alone determined capacity.

We also do not assume that managed support, application migration, backups, paid control-panel licenses or preinstalled website software are included unless the exact provider offer explicitly says so.

## Affiliate disclosure

Some “Choose a VPS” links are partner links. VPSforWebsite.com may earn a commission when a purchase is made through one of those links.

That does not change the server specifications we publish or the distinction between provider facts and our own sizing guidance.

## Review process

Commercial specifications and prices are checked against the provider before publication and rechecked when the site is updated. Technical recommendations are reviewed against the behavior of the actual workload rather than copied from generic “best hosting” lists.

Current provider specifications used on the English master site were reviewed on **12 October 2026**.

## Editorial principle

The smallest VPS that safely fits the real workload is usually a better starting point than buying excess capacity without measurement. Scale when monitoring shows a real constraint.
